What the 2025 Federal Budget Means for Small Business Owners

Tax Planning March 5, 2026 · By Huf McIntyre, CPA, CA

The 2025 Federal Budget introduced several notable changes to the Canadian tax landscape, particularly for small and medium-sized business owners. While many of the headlines focused on larger policy initiatives, there are specific provisions that will directly affect how you plan your corporate and personal tax affairs over the coming years.

Changes to the Small Business Deduction

One of the most significant changes involves adjustments to the small business deduction rate and the thresholds at which it begins to phase out. For business owners who have been relying on the lower tax rate available to Canadian-Controlled Private Corporations (CCPCs), it's important to understand how these changes affect your after-tax income and retained earnings.

The phase-out range for the small business deduction has been adjusted, meaning that businesses with taxable capital between certain thresholds will see a gradual reduction in their access to the lower rate. This is particularly relevant for growing businesses that are approaching or have recently exceeded the previous thresholds.

"The biggest mistake we see business owners make is treating budget announcements as distant policy — rather than understanding the direct, dollar-for-dollar impact on their bottom line."

Capital Gains Inclusion Rate

The budget confirmed changes to the capital gains inclusion rate that were first proposed in the previous fiscal year. For business owners considering a sale, succession plan, or significant asset disposition, the timing of these transactions relative to the implementation dates could have a substantial impact on your tax liability.

What this means in practice

  • Owners planning to sell their business should review the timeline with their accountant to optimize the tax treatment
  • Real estate held within corporate structures may be affected differently depending on the nature and timing of disposition
  • The Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption (LCGE) amount has been updated, providing some additional shelter for qualifying dispositions

Employee Ownership Trusts

The budget expanded provisions for Employee Ownership Trusts (EOTs), making it easier for business owners to transition ownership to their employees. This is a meaningful development for owners who are approaching retirement and want to ensure business continuity while receiving fair compensation for the value they've built.

What should you do now?

If you're a small business owner, the key takeaway is this: don't wait until year-end to assess how these changes affect your situation. Proactive planning — reviewing your corporate structure, compensation strategy, and any planned transactions — is the most effective way to minimize your tax exposure and take advantage of the opportunities these changes create.
